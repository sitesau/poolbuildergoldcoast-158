When considering a custom concrete pool on the Gold Coast, it's essential to view the project as property-led rather than a simple catalogue selection. Homeowners should begin by articulating their pool idea, specifying the suburb or postcode, assessing site access, understanding the property's slope, noting nearby structures, and considering connected outdoor works. This sequence is practical because a pool design that appears straightforward on paper can become intricate when factoring in excavation access, grade adjustments, drainage requirements, fencing, and retaining walls. A site-led brief ensures the initial discussions are grounded in reality. Instead of solely inquiring about pricing, it's more beneficial to outline the conditions that will influence the written scope. This includes identifying potential machinery entry points, the proximity of existing structures to the proposed pool shell, whether the land slopes across the yard, and which outdoor areas need to integrate seamlessly with the pool's edge. While photos can be provided later if requested by a builder, early written notes are invaluable for pinpointing the questions that require professional answers.

What a Written Scope Should Detail

Published guidance strongly advocates for a clear distinction between written inclusions, exclusions, and approval assumptions. This is particularly important for custom concrete pool construction, as the pool shell, surrounding areas, equipment, material selections, and compliance elements can be discussed collectively while still being priced and agreed upon individually. A well-defined scope should clearly delineate what is included in the quote, what is considered an allowance (subject to change), and what remains contingent on further site investigation or specific approvals.

  • Inclusions: These are the items the contractor commits to supplying or coordinating as part of the quoted scope.
  • Allowances: These represent items where the final cost or specification may vary once engineering, excavation, or specific selections are finalised.
  • Exclusions: These refer to associated works that the property owner might need to arrange independently.
  • Approval assumptions: This outlines the basis on which the proposal addresses pool approvals, fencing requirements, and surrounding works.

Approvals, Fencing, and Licence Verification

The planning process treats pool fencing and regulatory compliance as integral layout considerations rather than afterthoughts. This approach is practical, as barrier access and the design of surrounding works can significantly impact the pool's positioning and the flow of movement within the outdoor space. While specific regulations, thresholds, or approval timelines are not detailed, these should be confirmed for the particular project before the final layout is established. Verifying the contractor's credentials is also a critical part of early due diligence. Property owners are advised to confirm the proposed contractor's identity and their relevant QBCC licence before committing to any agreement. This check should be conducted concurrently with the review of the written scope, as understanding who is undertaking the work and the assumptions underpinning their proposal is vital. If any aspect concerning approvals, fencing, or licensing remains unclear, it should be explicitly documented in the project paperwork before comparing different proposals.

Cost Factors to Discuss Before Comparing Quotes

While the source material does not provide fixed pool pricing, it states that quotes, prices, rates, and estimates are indicative and subject to individual circumstances and potential revisions. However, it does identify key cost drivers that warrant discussion prior to comparing proposals. These include site access, excavation complexity, soil or rock conditions, engineering requirements, equipment selection, chosen finishes, and the scope of surrounding works. These elements are not minor details; they can fundamentally influence project practicality, the need for specialist input, and what should be included in the contract. It is important to ascertain whether excavation access has been assumed in the quote, if potential soil or rock risks have been accounted for, what pool equipment is included, which finishes are specified, and how connected outdoor works are addressed. A seemingly lower price can be misleading if one proposal includes an allowance for a particular item while another entirely excludes the same work.
